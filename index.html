Is the Annual Fee Worth It?

Premium travel credit cards often involve hundreds of dollars in credits, bonus points on every meal, and perks with fancy names. But a card with an annual fee is only worth it if its benefits fit the way you already live. A $120 credit at a store you would never visit is worth nothing to you.

As a student who is curious about personal finance and travel, I built this page to work through that question with you. I will compare three popular travel cards from three issuers so you can run the same comparison with your own spending habits.

Basic Concepts

Annual fee
What you pay each year to keep the card, regardless of whether you use it or not.
Points & miles
Rewards you earn by spending. Each issuer runs its own program, so points from different programs have different values.
Statement credit
Money taken off your bill after a qualifying purchase.
Travel portal
The issuer's own booking site, like Chase Travel. Some perks only work when you book there, not directly with the airline or hotel.
Redemption
Turning points into travel, gift cards, or cash. This is where the value of your points can vary a lot depending on how you redeem them.

The idea here is the difference between a benefit's "advertised amount" and its "personal value." The advertised amount is what the issuers say they will give you. Your personal value is what you would actually save based on how you normally spend without this card. For example, if a $100 hotel credit only helps when you book a trip you weren't planning, counting the full $100 hides the extra money you spent to get it.

Card Annual fee Points Multipliers

All three have no foreign transaction fees and let you redeem points for portal travel or transfer them to airline and hotel partners. One catch is that the transfer partners are different for every issuer.

Welcome Offers

Each card offers new cardholders a one-time bonus after early spending. The welcome offers are often the most valuable part of a card. However, these numbers do change from time to time, so this is just a snapshot of what was available when I checked the cards.

  • Chase Sapphire Preferred: 75,000 points after spending $5,000 in the first 3 months
  • American Express Gold: up to 100,000 points after spending $8,000 in the first 6 months
  • Citi Strata Premier: 60,000 points after spending $4,000 in the first 3 months

How to Compare Them

  1. Start with the annual fee. If you think it's too high, just don't get it.
  2. Identify benefits that match existing habits.
  3. Estimate their realistic yearly value specifically to your spending patterns.
  4. Using my calculator below, estimate the net benefit of each card.
  5. Consider rewards and redemption options separately (transfer partners).

Conclusion

Whether or not a card is worth it to you is personal. A $325 card can make sense for someone who uses its monthly credits without thinking, while a $95 card can be a waste for someone who doesn't spend more than $500 in a single hotel stay to get the $100 credit. Remember, you are never obligated to keep a card after the first year. You can always cancel it or downgrade to a no-annual-fee card if you find that it's not right for you.
